# Unsigned-varint transfer on engine 3b05c191

**The frozen canonical encoder property is blocked. No full encoder proof or exact inference certificate was obtained.** A separate, explicitly weaker result was proved: for every `u64` and arbitrary initialized exclusive `&mut [u8;10]`, the original encoder returns a slice of length 1–10 and satisfies its safety/debug assertions. That result requires authored loop annotations and bit-vector proof work; it is not unaided transfer or acceptance of the canonical goal.

This directory is fresh research evidence. It does not change `evidence/verus-infer-transfer-v1` on cached ref `origin/t3/publish-transfer-evidence`, or any compiler, macro, vstd, dependency or common-transfer runner source. No parent checkout/artifacts were rebuilt or modified, and nothing was pushed.

The actual first outcomes, in separate cold and warm processes with and without `--infer-goals`, were:

- The unadapted macro is rejected for a macro-produced `for` loop. Nothing downstream is established by that rejection.
- The adapted inferred body, including the caller arrangement, is rejected at the actual `core::slice::iter_mut` call: inference requires an inferred callee or certified imported summary. No candidate or soundness/maximality/exactness result for this encoder exists. Possible later signature/loop limitations are not reported as observed failures.
- The adapted ordinary body reaches index-range, counter-overflow and debug-assertion obligations. Its historical authored `global size_of usize == 8` declaration is also rejected by `--no-cheating`. Those frozen trials remain intact.
- `layout-refinement.json` records a separate variant removing only that layout declaration, without adding any platform premise or changing the executable bodies, goal or domain. With `--multiple-errors 20`, ordinary checking reports the canonical postcondition failure as well as the three safety failures. This is an observed proof failure, not a frontend error or an application defect.

`manual/canonical-attempt.rs` adds four scalar/iterator invariants, a loop postcondition, an initial shift identity, and a `shift_step` lemma called per iteration. The lemma contains two checked bit-vector assertions; the initial identity adds a third. There are no application assumptions, admitted bodies, preconditions on the encoder, or verification bypasses. This discharges safety, but the full canonical postcondition still fails, both ordinarily and with `--infer-goals`. A written-prefix invariant relating iterator referents to payload, continuation and radix/minimality facts has not been established.

`manual/length-attempt.rs` changes only the authored goal to the separately named partial result. It reports **8 verified, 0 errors**, over the full crate, ordinarily and with `--infer-goals`, cold and warm. The exclusive-range mutant in `manual/length-negative.rs` changes exactly `[0..=i]` to `[0..i]`; it fails only its authored length postcondition. `check_results.py` strips the explicit proof additions and checks that the positive executable body is exactly the layout-free original adapter, and that the negative has precisely that mutation. This smaller theorem does not replace the frozen canonical acceptance criterion.

Independent controls verify the macro-adapter example and reject a false return postcondition. The authored canonical predicate has proved one-byte and two-byte-boundary laws and separate payload, continuation, minimality, empty and excessive-length negative controls. These test the semantic definition, not the encoder. An inferred scalar mask control passes independent mandatory soundness, maximality and exactness checks, including fresh ordinary caller checking. No such control is counted as an encoder certificate.

Native debug and release runs each passed three suites totaling **173,503 input/buffer pairs**: exhaustive low 16-bit values, all radix boundaries with all uniform byte patterns, and deterministic full-width samples with independently filled buffers. They compare the full upstream module, original selected body, actual macro adapter and actual erased authored-length variant. They also check the untouched suffix and that the returned pointer is the buffer start. They use the real matching macro dependency, not an identity macro. These are finite supporting oracles; they prove neither universal behavior nor compiler/adapter equivalence, suffix preservation or alias identity.
